Retail analytics & customer segmentation

An interactive dashboard that tracks retail KPIs and segments customers by RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary), so a team can see who its best customers are and where revenue comes from.

Type
Personal project · dashboard
Stack
Python, Pandas, Streamlit, Plotly
Data
Synthetic (12,000 orders, 800 customers)
Links
Live demo ↗ · GitHub ↗

The question

Which customers are worth the most, which are slipping away, and where does revenue actually come from? A dashboard for a business or data analyst should answer those questions without anyone writing a query.

About the data. The dataset is synthetic. The repository includes the generator script, which creates 12,000 orders across 800 customers with varying purchase frequency to mimic real loyalty patterns. That makes the project reproducible without real customer data.

What the dashboard shows

  • KPI overview: total revenue, order count, average order value, and active customers.
  • Trend analysis: weekly revenue trend and revenue broken down by category, channel, and region.
  • RFM segmentation: every customer is scored on how recently they bought, how often, and how much they spend, then classified into a segment.
  • Filters: date range, region, category, and channel.

The segments

SegmentWho they are
ChampionsRecent, frequent, high spend
Loyal CustomersBuy regularly and reliably
Potential LoyalistsRecent buyers who could become loyal
At RiskUsed to buy often but haven't lately
Lost / DormantHave stopped buying

Why RFM

RFM is one of the most widely used segmentation techniques in retail and e-commerce analytics because it is simple, easy to explain, and directly actionable. At Risk customers are exactly who a retention campaign should target, and Champions are who a loyalty program should reward.

Limits and next steps

  • The data is synthetic, so the segment sizes illustrate the method and are not business findings.
  • With real data, I would check how the segment boundaries were chosen against actual purchase behavior before acting on them.
  • Natural additions would be a customer lifetime value view and a cohort retention chart.
